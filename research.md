# Why the Death Stranding Loop Is Addicting — Design Rules for a Distillation

Companion to `research-death-stranding-delivery-mechanics.md` (systems and numbers). This doc: the psychology, and the rules the new game must follow.

## 1. Plan → struggle → payoff

Engagement starts before movement: studying the map, plotting around danger, choosing cargo where every kilogram changes handling. The traversal itself is the content — weight distribution, an actively-corrected balance meter, stamina, terrain reading give constant low-level skill expression. Effort justification is the engine: PC Gamer's DS2 critique shows the negative proof — when gear removes balance and consequence, "struggle justified the eventual payoff" breaks and satisfaction drains.

DESIGN RULES: real planning phase (map + loadout that visibly changes movement); traversal demands micro-inputs (wobble corrections, stamina tiles, slope costs); every convenience is paid for after beating the problem the hard way first; every choice priced as a trade-off (more cargo = better reward + worse handling), no dominant strategy.

## 2. Stack every feedback channel

DS lands six channels on one delivery: evaluation grade card, likes burst (likes are the XP and the payment), facility stars 1–5, chiral network % visibly claiming the map, porter-grade titles, and the scripted music swell on final approach (HUD fades, camera pulls out, track swells) — the moment players cite most. Strongest payoff overall: before/after contrast ("the roads hit me like a drug"; 47,000 delayed likes from rebuilt roads).

DESIGN RULES: ship all six channels; script the approach-swell on every delivery; pay likes on a delay ("while you were resting, 3 porters used your bridge — +36") — delayed recognition of old effort is the retention hook.

## 3. Infrastructure = investment, not chore

The tool ladder re-expresses mastery in new verbs: place-in-crisis (ladder) → place-ahead (bridge, shelter) → route-design (zipline pairs) → network-design (roads as communal megaproject sinks). Trivializing a region is fine because it was purchased with visible effort — but the game must then open a harsher region so mastery re-expresses instead of idling. Decay (timefall) exists to create return visits and gratitude, never punishment: slow decay, one-button repair.

DESIGN RULES: one new build-verb every 1–2 hours early; big infrastructure = multi-trip material sink with a visible progress bar at the site; decay slow + repair cheap; when a region is trivialized, open a harsher one with orders too big for old methods.

## 4. Warmth without contact

Likes work where social media fails: they attach to the useful object itself, awarded by the people it helped, positivity-only, no griefing surface, per-object caps. The core emotion is a stranger's ladder at the exact right moment — and learning your bridge saved someone. Offline DS proves the system is fakeable: NPC porters, delayed gratitude mail, seeded structures still produce the warmth.

DESIGN RULES: simulate echo porters (they use your structures overnight → like bursts; they leave their own ladders in dark regions); engineered serendipity (spawn an echo structure near a spot the player repeatedly failed); positivity only; delayed thank-you mail referencing the specific delivery.

## 5. Pacing

DS1's documented drop-offs: 90 min before meaningful play, real tools gated until Episode 3. DS2 was rebuilt to remove drop-off points. The "one more delivery" hook is mechanical: orders always available, batchable along one route, next facility always visible, timed/premium variants give replay value on known routes. Threats staggered one at a time with safe corridors between.

DESIGN RULES: first delivery within 3 minutes; loop fully demonstrated (plan→walk→grade→likes) inside 30; never let the order queue empty (3+ concurrent, one batchable); introduce one threat at a time, telegraphed on the map; sand down minute-one and the first difficulty spike.

## 6. Pitfalls to design against

Repetition of identical A-to-B structure; menu/inventory friction ("selecting tools was more of a chore than it should be"); weak combat and unloved bosses; exploits that void the core (DS1: holding both triggers erased the balance game); DS2's over-correction — equipment bloat that removes the need for skill.

DESIGN RULES: vary order constraints (timed, fragile, oversized, multi-stop), not just endpoints; one-button batch pickup/dropoff + radial quick-select; combat minimal, non-lethal, avoidance-first — cargo is the stake, never health; no bosses; bracing costs stamina and speed so it can't be held permanently.
